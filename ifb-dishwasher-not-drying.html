IFB Dishwasher Not Drying Dishes Properly

Dishes come out clean but still wet, sometimes with water pooling in cups and bowls — here's what's actually behind it.

Quick answer: Wet dishes after a complete wash cycle usually point to one of three things — low or empty rinse aid, a faulty heating element used for the dry cycle, or a blocked vent that lets steam escape instead of condensing properly.

Why Drying Fails Even When Washing Works Fine

Dishwashers dry dishes using a combination of heat and rinse aid — rinse aid causes water to sheet off surfaces rather than forming droplets, while a heating element (or condensation drying in some models) removes remaining moisture. A fault in either part shows up specifically as a drying problem, even though the wash and rinse stages worked completely normally.

Common Causes

Empty or Low Rinse Aid

Without rinse aid, water beads and pools instead of sheeting off — the single most common cause of "clean but wet" dishes.

Heating Element Fault

The element used during the drying phase can fail independently of the wash-cycle heating function.

Blocked Vent

Some models release steam through a vent during drying — if blocked, moisture stays trapped inside.

Overloading the Rack

Overloaded racks block air/heat circulation, leaving items in shadowed spots still damp.

Plastic Items Retaining Moisture

Plastic doesn't retain heat like ceramic or steel, so it stays wet longer — this is somewhat normal and not always a fault.

What a Proper Diagnosis Looks Like

  • Check and top up the rinse aid dispenser first — resolves the majority of cases
  • Confirm loading isn't blocking air/heat circulation
  • Test the heating element for the dry cycle specifically
  • Check the vent (if the model has one) for blockage
Avoid this mistake: Assuming a drying complaint means the heating element has failed, without first checking rinse aid levels — an empty rinse aid dispenser is behind a large share of these complaints and costs nothing to refill.

Can You Check This Yourself First?

Open the rinse aid compartment (usually near the detergent dispenser) and confirm it isn't empty — top it up if needed. Also make sure racks aren't overloaded in a way that blocks water/heat circulation. These two checks resolve the majority of drying complaints.

Is This Covered Under Warranty?

If your IFB dishwasher is still under company warranty, a heating element fault is typically covered. Once warranty has expired, an independent repair with genuine parts and our own 3-month service warranty is usually faster.
