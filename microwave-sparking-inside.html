Microwave Sparking Inside? Here's What's Causing It and What To Do Right Now

Sparking is one microwave issue you shouldn't wait on — here's why it happens and the safe first step.

📟 Not sure if it’s this exact issue? Try our free Microwave Checker →
Quick answer: Stop using the microwave immediately. Sparking is almost always caused by damage to the metal mesh/waveguide cover, a metal object inside, or a worn mica plate — all of which are fixable, but shouldn't be run again until checked.
First step — always: Unplug the microwave and don't use it again until it's inspected. Continuing to run a sparking microwave can damage the magnetron (the most expensive part) or, in rare cases, pose a fire risk.

Common Causes of Sparking

Damaged Waveguide Cover

The mica/waveguide cover protects internal components from food splatter — if it's burnt or damaged, sparking is common.

Metal Object Inside

Metal utensils, twist ties, or foil accidentally left inside will spark immediately when microwaved.

Food/Grease Buildup

Carbonized food residue on interior walls can conduct and spark similarly to metal.

Worn Turntable Support

Less common — a damaged support ring can occasionally cause arcing.

What To Check Before Calling a Technician

Two quick, safe checks you can do yourself:

  • Look for a metal object — check thoroughly for any utensils, foil, or metal-rimmed containers
  • Inspect the waveguide cover (the small mica/ceramic panel usually on a side or back wall) — if it's visibly burnt, cracked, or has holes, that's likely the cause

If neither applies, the cause needs a proper internal inspection — don't keep testing by running the microwave repeatedly, as this risks further damage.

Is the Waveguide Cover Replacement Expensive?

No — it's actually one of the more affordable microwave repairs. The mistake to avoid is ignoring the sparking and continuing to use the microwave, which can damage the magnetron and turn an affordable fix into an expensive one.

Is This Covered Under Warranty?

If your microwave is still under company warranty, this is typically covered. If the warranty has expired, an independent repair with genuine parts is usually fast and affordable — waveguide covers are inexpensive parts to replace.
