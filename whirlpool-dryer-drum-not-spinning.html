Whirlpool Dryer Drum Not Spinning

You can hear the motor running, maybe even a faint humming, but the drum itself stays still — here's what's actually broken.

🌬️ Not sure if it’s this exact issue? Try our free Dryer Self Check →
Quick answer: A motor that runs (or hums) while the drum stays still almost always means a broken or slipped drive belt — the motor is disconnected from the drum mechanically, even though it's still receiving power correctly.

Why the Motor Runs But the Drum Doesn't Turn

Most dryers connect the motor to the drum using a long rubber drive belt looped around a pulley system. If that belt snaps or slips off its track, the motor continues running completely normally — since electrically nothing has changed — but there's no longer a mechanical connection moving the drum.

Common Causes

Broken Drive Belt

The most common cause by far — belts wear out and snap with age and heavy use.

Slipped Belt

The belt can come off its pulley track without breaking, especially after an overloaded cycle.

Worn Drum Rollers or Bearings

Support rollers that the drum rests on can seize up, adding enough resistance to stall rotation.

Motor Failure

Less common if the motor is audibly running, but a weak motor can fail to generate enough torque to turn a loaded drum.

Door Switch Fault

Some models won't engage the drum motor fully if the door switch isn't signalling correctly, even if the door is shut.

What a Proper Diagnosis Looks Like

  • Check for a broken or slipped belt first — visible with the back or front panel removed
  • Inspect drum support rollers and bearings for seizing or wear
  • Test the door switch signal if belt and rollers are intact
  • Test motor torque and function directly only after the above are ruled out
Avoid this mistake: Continuing to run cycles with a snapped belt can cause the motor to overheat since it's spinning freely with no load resistance — get it checked promptly rather than repeatedly trying cycles.

Is This Safe to Check Yourself?

Visually checking if you can see or hear the belt (a faint slapping/rattling sound often means a loose belt) is fine, but replacing the belt itself requires opening the dryer's casing and correctly re-routing it around the pulley system — we recommend a technician for the actual repair.

Is This Covered Under Warranty?

If your Whirlpool dryer is still under company warranty, a belt or motor fault is typically covered. Once warranty has expired, an independent repair with genuine parts and our own 3-month service warranty is usually faster than the manufacturer's service queue.
