Water pooling inside or under the fridge usually traces back to one of a few specific points.

Quick answer: Water leaking from a Whirlpool refrigerator is most commonly caused by a blocked defrost drain, a damaged door gasket, or (in Mumbai's humid climate) condensation building up faster than it can drain properly.
⚠️ Safety first: Agar fridge bilkul thanda nahi ho raha ya gas smell/leak lag rahi hai, toh turant power off karke door band rakhe — taaki compressor par load na pade aur bacha hua saaman kharab na ho.

Where Is the Water Actually Coming From?

The first useful question is location: is the water inside the fridge (pooling under crisper drawers), or outside underneath the unit? This narrows down the cause significantly before any technician even arrives.

Common Causes

Blocked Defrost Drain

Ice or food debris blocks the small drain hole at the back of the fridge, causing water to back up and pool inside.

Damaged Door Gasket

A torn or loose seal lets humid air in constantly, increasing condensation and moisture buildup.

Cracked Drain Pan

The pan underneath that collects defrost water can crack over time, leaking onto the floor.

Excess Humidity (Mumbai Climate)

Mumbai's high humidity increases condensation load on the cooling system, especially if the door seal is even slightly worn.

Water Filter or Dispenser Line Issue

If your model has a water dispenser, a loose connection or damaged line can leak from the front.

What to Check First

  • Check if the leak is from the front (dispenser) or the back/bottom (drain-related)
  • Run a hand along the door seal — feel for gaps, tears, or looseness
  • If safe to do so, check the drain hole at the back of the interior for visible ice blockage

If none of these are obvious, the issue is likely inside the drain pan or defrost system — that's when a proper inspection makes sense rather than guessing further.

Avoid this mistake: Ignoring a slow, small leak because it seems minor — over time, water pooling near electrical components or causing floor damage turns a small fix into a bigger repair.
