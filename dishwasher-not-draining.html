Dishwasher Not Draining? Here's What's Blocking It

Standing water at the bottom after every cycle usually points to one of a few specific blockages.

Quick answer: A dishwasher not draining almost always means something is physically blocking the drain path — most commonly the filter, but sometimes the drain hose or pump. It's rarely a sign of a major mechanical failure.

Why Standing Water Happens

Dishwashers drain through a filter (which catches food debris), then through a hose to the drain pump, which pushes water out. A blockage anywhere in this path leaves water sitting at the bottom after a cycle finishes — and unlike a washing machine, dishwashers process food particles constantly, so their filters need more regular attention.

Common Causes

Blocked Filter

Food debris accumulates here over time — this is the single most common cause by far.

Kinked or Blocked Drain Hose

The hose behind the unit can bend or collect debris over time.

Garbage Disposal Connection Blocked

If connected to a garbage disposal or sink drain, a clog there backs up into the dishwasher too.

Faulty Drain Pump

Less common — the pump motor itself can fail or get jammed by a foreign object.

Air Gap Blockage

If your installation has an air gap (small cylinder near the sink), it can clog and prevent proper draining.

What to Check First

The filter is usually at the bottom of the dishwasher tub, under the lower spray arm, and twists out for cleaning in most models:

  • Remove the filter and rinse away any trapped food debris
  • Check the drain hose (if accessible) for visible kinks
  • If connected near a kitchen sink, check that the sink drain itself isn't clogged

If the dishwasher still isn't draining after clearing the filter, the blockage is likely further down — that's when a proper inspection is worth it rather than guessing further.

Avoid this mistake: Replacing the drain pump before checking the filter and hose is one of the most common ways this repair gets more expensive than it needs to be — the filter is the cause in the large majority of cases.
