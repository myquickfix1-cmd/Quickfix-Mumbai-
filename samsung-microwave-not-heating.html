Quick answer: When everything about a microwave seems to work — turntable spinning, display lit, timer counting down — except actual heating, the fault is almost always the magnetron, the high-voltage diode, or a door interlock switch not signalling correctly to the control board.
⚠️ Safety first: Agar andar spark ho raha hai ya plastic/burning smell aa rahi hai, toh turant unplug kar de aur microwave chalana band kar de — taaki magnetron ya fuse poori tarah fuk na jaye.

Why the Microwave "Runs" But Doesn't Heat

The turntable motor, display, and timer are on a separate low-power circuit from the actual microwave-generating components — the magnetron and its high-voltage supply. This is exactly why a microwave can appear to be working completely normally on the outside while producing zero actual heat.

🔥 Microwave Runs But Not Heating

Magnetron failure, high-voltage diode short, or faulty transformer preventing heating.

⚡ Sparking Inside / Burning Smell

Damaged mica wave-guide cover, burnt paint, or shorted magnetron nozzle causing arcing.

👆 Touchpad / Buttons Not Working

Membrane switch failure, ribbon cable rust, or control panel circuit breakdown (SE error code).

🔄 Plate Not Rotating / Jerky Motion

Turntable coupler broken, motor gear stripped, or drive belt slip under the glass plate.

❌ Microwave Completely Dead / No Display

Main glass fuse blown, line filter switch shorted, or main PCB power section failure.

🚪 Door Lock / Latch Sensor Fault

Primary/secondary door switch misaligned, latch hook broken, or door safety interlock open.

📟 PCB Error Codes (SE, E-11, 5E)

Sensors failure, display controller bug, or voltage surge damages to main PCB circuit board.

🔊 Loud Humming or Buzzing Noise

High-voltage capacitor failure, cooling fan motor stuck, or transformer coil vibration.

What a Proper Diagnosis Looks Like

  • Check door interlock switches are engaging properly first — this is a safety-critical, testable component
  • Test the high-voltage diode and capacitor with proper equipment
  • Test the magnetron itself only after ruling out the supporting components
  • Check control board signal if all individual components test healthy
Important safety note: Microwave high-voltage components can hold a dangerous charge even when unplugged. This is not a safe DIY repair — always have a qualified technician handle internal diagnosis and part replacement.
