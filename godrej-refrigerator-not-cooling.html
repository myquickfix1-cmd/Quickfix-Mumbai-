Quick answer: A Godrej refrigerator not cooling usually points to one of four things — low or leaked refrigerant gas, a dust-choked condenser coil, a faulty thermostat, or a worn door seal letting cold air escape. The compressor itself is rarely the actual cause.
⚠️ Safety first: Agar fridge bilkul thanda nahi ho raha ya gas smell/leak lag rahi hai, toh turant power off karke door band rakhe — taaki compressor par load na pade aur bacha hua saaman kharab na ho.

Godrej Refrigerator Not Cooling But Light Is On & Compressor Running

One of the most common misunderstandings is assuming a running compressor means everything is fine. The compressor is just one part of the cooling loop — it can run continuously while gas has leaked out, the condenser is too dusty to release heat, or the thermostat isn't sensing temperature correctly. Cooling failure is usually a systems issue, not a "the compressor died" issue.

In Mumbai's dusty, humid conditions, condenser coils — usually at the back or bottom of the fridge — collect dust and grime noticeably faster than in drier climates, making this one of the most frequent causes we diagnose on service visits here.

Model-Wise Causes: Single Door, Double Door (Frost Free) & Godrej Eon

Refrigerant Gas Leakage

A slow leak reduces cooling gradually — compressor runs, but the cooling cycle can't complete properly.

Dust-Choked Condenser Coil

Blocked coils can't release heat efficiently, especially common in Mumbai's dusty environment.

Faulty Thermostat

Misreads internal temperature, so the compressor cycles incorrectly or stops too early.

Worn Door Seal / Gasket

Lets warm, humid air in continuously — compressor overworks but can't keep up.

Defrost System Fault

Ice buildup on the evaporator coil blocks airflow, reducing cooling to the fridge compartment.

Overloading or Blocked Vents

Packed shelves blocking internal air vents restrict circulation, causing uneven or reduced cooling.

What a Proper Diagnosis Looks Like

Because several faults share identical symptoms, isolating the real cause matters before any part is replaced:

  • Check condenser coil for dust buildup and clean if needed — a very common, low-cost fix
  • Inspect door seals for gaps or wear using a simple paper-slip test
  • Check for ice buildup on the evaporator, indicating a defrost system fault
  • Test thermostat response against actual internal temperature
  • Only after ruling out the above is gas pressure tested for a possible leak
Avoid this mistake: Topping up refrigerant gas without finding and sealing the actual leak point means the gas will leak out again within weeks — always locate the leak first, not just recharge the gas.

Can You Check This Yourself First?

A few things worth checking before booking a repair:

  • Check the door seal — close it on a piece of paper and see if it slides out easily (a sign of a worn gasket)
  • Look at the condenser coil (usually rear or bottom-front) for visible dust buildup and clean gently if accessible
  • Avoid overpacking shelves in front of internal air vents

If cooling is still poor after these checks, the cause is likely gas leakage, thermostat, or the defrost system — that needs a proper diagnostic visit.
