Quick answer: A washing machine won't start its cycle unless it can confirm the door is securely locked — a door that "closes" but doesn't latch or signal correctly is usually a worn latch mechanism or a faulty door lock sensor, not a major electrical fault.

Why the Door Lock Matters So Much

Front-load washing machines hold several litres of water at height during a cycle — the door lock is a genuine safety mechanism, not just a convenience feature. Because of this, the machine's control board will refuse to start a cycle at all until it receives a clear "door secured" signal, even if the door physically looks shut.

Common Causes

Worn Door Latch Mechanism

The physical latch that engages when the door closes can wear down with years of use, no longer triggering properly.

Faulty Door Lock Sensor

The electronic sensor that confirms the lock is engaged can fail even when the latch itself works fine.

Misaligned Door Hinge

A slightly misaligned door won't seat correctly against the latch, preventing proper engagement.

Damaged Wiring to the Lock

Wear on the wiring harness near the door area can interrupt the signal to the control board.

Control Board Fault

Less common — the board itself fails to read a working sensor's signal correctly.

What a Proper Diagnosis Looks Like

  • Check the door closes flush and the hinge alignment is correct
  • Inspect the latch mechanism for visible wear or damage
  • Test the door lock sensor's signal directly with diagnostic tools
  • Check wiring continuity between the sensor and control board
  • Only after ruling out the above is the control board itself tested
Avoid this mistake: Forcing the door shut harder or repeatedly slamming it to try to trigger the lock can damage the latch mechanism further — if it's not engaging within a couple of normal attempts, it needs a proper inspection.

Can You Check This Yourself First?

Check that nothing (a stray sock, detergent residue) is obstructing the door seal or latch area, and confirm the door is closing flush without gaps. If it still won't lock after a clean, gentle close, the sensor or latch mechanism likely needs inspection.

Is This Covered Under Warranty?

If your machine is still under company warranty, a door lock sensor fault is typically a straightforward warranty claim. Once warranty has expired, an independent repair with genuine parts and our own 3-month service warranty is usually quicker.
